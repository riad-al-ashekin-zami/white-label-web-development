# What Is White Label Web Development?

White label web development is a business model where one company builds websites or handles development work on behalf of another company, while the finished work is delivered under the purchasing company's brand.

For a step-by-step explanation, read [how white label web development works](/blog/how-does-white-label-web-development-work).

In a typical arrangement, a digital marketing agency, SEO company, design studio, consultant, or freelancer sells a website project to its client. Instead of completing every technical task internally, the agency sends the project to a **white label web development partner**.

The development partner works behind the scenes.

The agency remains the face of the project, manages the client relationship, and delivers the finished website under its own brand.

In simple terms:

> **Your agency gets the client → your white label partner builds the website → you deliver it under your brand.**

This model allows agencies to offer professional web development without necessarily hiring a full internal development department.

But white label development is more than simply sending a project to a random freelancer.

A proper white label relationship normally requires clear project ownership, confidentiality, defined communication channels, quality assurance, predictable delivery processes, and an agreement about how the development partner interacts—or does not interact—with the end client.

This guide explains **what white label web development is, how it works, what services can be white labeled, how agencies make money from it, what it can cost, how it compares with freelancers and in-house development, and what to look for when choosing a white label development partner.**

## Quick Answer: What Is White Label Web Development?

**White label web development is when an external developer or development company builds websites for another agency to sell and deliver under its own brand.**

The agency usually owns the client relationship, handles sales and account management, and determines what it charges the client. The white label development partner handles agreed technical work behind the scenes.

For example, an SEO agency may sell a $5,000 website project to a client and hire a white label development partner to complete the development portion of that project. The client continues working with the SEO agency, while the development team operates in the background according to the agreed white-label arrangement.

The model can help agencies expand their services and delivery capacity without immediately adding permanent development headcount.

# A Simple White Label Development Example

Imagine that you run an SEO agency.

Your core team consists of:

- SEO strategists
- content writers
- link builders
- account managers

One of your existing clients asks:

> "Can you redesign and develop our website as well?"

You now have several options.

You could decline the project.

You could refer the client to another development company.

You could hire developers.

You could try to coordinate several freelancers.

Or you could use a **white label web development service**.

With the white label model, your agency can remain responsible for the overall engagement while a specialist development team handles the website production.

A simplified workflow could be:

**Client requests website → Agency scopes project → White label partner reviews technical requirements → Agency provides proposal → Development begins → Agency reviews work → Client provides feedback → Revisions are completed → Website launches**

The important point is that your agency remains at the center of the relationship.

# How Does White Label Web Development Work?

Although every partnership is different, a structured white label project commonly follows several stages.

## Step 1: Your team Wins the Client

Your agency handles:

- marketing
- sales
- discovery
- client relationship
- account management

You identify that the client requires website development.

---

## Step 2: Requirements Are Collected

The project needs to be defined before development begins.

Typical requirements include:

- number of pages
- design requirements
- CMS
- functionality
- forms
- integrations
- ecommerce requirements
- responsive requirements
- content availability
- SEO requirements
- launch date

Weak requirements create problems regardless of whether development is completed internally or externally.

---

## Step 3: The White Label Partner Reviews the Scope

The development partner evaluates the technical requirements.

They may identify:

- development complexity
- required technologies
- integrations
- potential technical limitations
- estimated delivery time
- dependencies
- expected development effort

The agency can then prepare its final client proposal.

---

## Step 4: The team Sets the Client Price

One of the major characteristics of white labeling is that the agency generally controls its own commercial relationship with the end client.

For example:

**Development partner cost:** $2,000

**Agency client price:** $4,000

The $2,000 difference should not automatically be interpreted as pure profit.

The agency may still be responsible for:

- sales
- project management
- meetings
- revisions
- content
- strategy
- quality assurance
- client support
- taxes
- software
- marketing
- other overhead

But white labeling allows the agency to create its own pricing structure instead of merely collecting a referral commission.

---

## Step 5: Development Begins

The partner completes the agreed work.

Depending on the arrangement, this may include:

- frontend development
- backend development
- CMS configuration
- responsive implementation
- integrations
- ecommerce
- testing
- performance optimization

The partner may work inside the agency's project management system or follow an agreed external workflow.

---

## Step 6: Internal QA

Before presenting the website to the client, both development QA and agency-level review may be necessary.

Typical checks include:

- desktop responsiveness
- mobile responsiveness
- tablet layouts
- forms
- navigation
- links
- images
- content
- browser compatibility
- basic accessibility
- page speed
- tracking
- SEO implementation
- functionality

A reliable white label process should not treat the end client as the QA department.

---

## Step 7: Client Feedback

Your agency collects feedback from the client.

That feedback is converted into actionable development tasks and passed to the development team.

This communication layer is extremely important.

Unstructured feedback creates scope creep and delays.

---

## Step 8: Revisions

The development partner completes agreed revisions.

A professional engagement should define:

- number of revision rounds
- what counts as a revision
- what constitutes new scope
- who approves changes
- how additional work is priced

---

## Step 9: Launch

After final approval, the website is deployed.

The exact launch process depends on the technology and hosting environment.

## Step 10: Ongoing Support

The relationship does not necessarily end after launch.

Clients may require:

- security updates
- backups

- content changes

# White Label Web Development vs Outsourcing

The two terms overlap, but they should not always be treated as identical.

White label development is generally a **specific form of outsourcing** structured around the purchasing company's brand and client relationship.

| Area | White Label Development | General Outsourcing |
|---|---|---|
| External team performs work | Yes | Yes |
| Agency branding | Central to model | Not necessarily |
| Partner visibility | Usually restricted | May be visible |
| Direct client relationship | Usually retained by agency | Varies |
| Confidentiality | Often formalized | Varies |
| Reselling | Core use case | Not always |
| Long-term partnership | Common | May be project-based |

The distinction becomes important when client ownership matters.

If you simply introduce another developer to your customer, you may be outsourcing or referring work.

If the development company operates as a behind-the-scenes extension of your agency under an agreed white-label structure, you are much closer to a true white label model.

# White Label Development vs Freelancers

Freelancers can be excellent.

The question is not whether freelancers are good or bad.

The question is whether the delivery model fits your agency.

| Factor | White Label Agency/Team | Individual Freelancer |
|---|---|---|
| Capacity | Potentially multiple developers | Usually one person |
| Project management | Often structured | Varies |
| QA resources | Can be dedicated | Often self-managed |
| Specialist availability | Multiple skill sets possible | Limited to individual |
| Scalability | Generally higher | Limited by personal capacity |
| Continuity | Team can provide redundancy | Individual dependency |
| Cost | May be higher | Can be lower |
| Flexibility | High | High |

A freelancer may be ideal for occasional, specialized work.

A structured white label team may make more sense when you need predictable capacity across multiple client projects.

# White Label Development vs In-House Developers

This is one of the biggest strategic decisions for a growing agency.

## In-House Development

An internal developer provides:

- direct communication
- deeper company familiarity
- consistent availability
- tighter cultural integration

But the agency also assumes:

- salary
- benefits
- recruitment
- equipment
- training
- management
- utilization risk

If project volume falls, payroll usually does not disappear.

## White Label Development

White labeling can turn at least part of development delivery into a variable project cost.

You buy capacity when needed.

But you also give up some direct control and must build a reliable external workflow.

### Comparison

| Factor | White Label Partner | In-House Team |
|---|---|---|
| Recruitment | Minimal | Required |
| Fixed payroll | Lower | Higher |
| Scale up/down | Easier | Harder |
| Direct control | Moderate | High |
| Team integration | Requires process | Strong |
| Specialist access | Potentially broad | Depends on hires |
| Idle capacity risk | Lower | Higher |
| Long-term internal knowledge | Lower | Higher |

Neither approach is automatically superior.

Many mature agencies use both.

They maintain a core internal team and use trusted partners for:

- overflow
- specialized projects
- temporary capacity
- services outside their core stack

# Potential Risks and Disadvantages

White label development is not automatically successful.

A poor partner can create more problems than they solve.

## Communication Risk

If your client tells you one thing and your team tells the developer something different, mistakes multiply.

You need a clear communication process.

---

## Quality Control

Your brand appears on the finished product.

That means the partner's mistake becomes your agency's problem.

QA cannot be optional.

---

## Missed Deadlines

If the development partner misses a deadline, your agency still has to explain it to the client.

This is why delivery history and project management matter.

---

## Scope Creep

A vague scope can destroy project margins.

Define:

- pages
- features
- integrations
- revisions
- responsibilities
- exclusions

before development begins.

---

## Confidentiality Risk

The development partner may see:

- client information
- credentials
- designs
- strategy
- business information

Appropriate contracts and access controls are therefore important.

---

## Vendor Dependency

If your entire delivery operation depends on one external company, disruption at that company can affect you.

Maintain:

- documentation
- backups
- account ownership
- repository access where applicable
- handover procedures

# How teams Make Money With White Label Development

The basic commercial model is straightforward.

### Example

Your white label partner quotes:

**$2,500**

Your agency sells the project for:

**$5,000**

Gross difference:

**$2,500**

But remember:

**Gross difference ≠ net profit.**

The agency may still incur:

- sales costs
- project management
- design
- account management
- revision management
- software
- payment fees
- overhead

The correct client price should therefore be based on the **total value and total delivery cost**, not simply:

`Partner cost + arbitrary percentage`

---

## Example Revenue Model

Suppose an agency sells four website projects per month.

Average client price:

**$5,000**

Monthly website revenue:

**4 × $5,000 = $20,000**

Suppose outsourced development averages:

**$2,500 × 4 = $10,000**

That leaves:

**$10,000**

before the agency's remaining operating costs.

This is only a hypothetical example, but it illustrates the model.

White label development can create margin because the agency owns:

- acquisition
- positioning
- strategy
- relationship
- project management
- final delivery

while purchasing technical production capacity separately.

# Who Owns the Website and Code?

Never assume.

Ownership should be addressed contractually.

Depending on the project, there may be several categories of intellectual property:

### Custom Work

Code or design produced specifically for the project.

### Open-Source Software

WordPress, plugins, libraries and other software may operate under their own licenses.

### Commercial Software

Premium themes, plugins, fonts or SaaS tools can have separate licensing conditions.

### Client Assets

Logos, photography, content and brand assets may already belong to the client.

Your agreements should explain:

- who owns custom deliverables
- when ownership transfers
- what third-party licenses apply
- who owns accounts
- what happens after termination

Do not rely on verbal assumptions for commercially important projects.

# How to Choose a White Label Web Development Partner

Selecting a white label partner should be treated more seriously than hiring someone to complete a single task.

Their work affects your reputation.

## 1. Review Their Portfolio

Look beyond attractive screenshots.

Ask:

- Are the websites live?
- Are they responsive?
- Do they load well?
- Is the implementation consistent?
- Have they handled projects similar to yours?

You can review examples of our own work in our [white label development portfolio](https://whitelabelwebsitedevelopment.com/portfolio).

---

## 2. Evaluate Technical Capabilities

Determine whether their expertise matches your pipeline.

Examples:

- WordPress
- WooCommerce
- frontend development
- custom development
- API integrations
- performance optimization

Do not choose a partner for hypothetical capabilities you may never need.

Choose for the projects you actually sell.

---

## 3. Evaluate Communication

Ask:

- Who is the point of contact?
- What are normal response times?
- How are urgent issues handled?
- What project management system is used?
- How is feedback submitted?

Strong development with poor communication can still create a poor agency experience.

---

## 4. Ask About QA

A development partner should have a repeatable process for checking its own work.

Ask about:

- browser testing
- mobile testing
- forms
- functionality
- performance
- content checks
- pre-launch review

---

## 5. Ask About Capacity

A developer who can handle one website perfectly may not be able to handle ten simultaneously.

If your objective is scaling, understand:

- concurrent project capacity
- team size
- specialization
- backup resources
- typical turnaround

---

## 6. Understand the Revision Process

Ask:

- How many rounds are included?
- What qualifies as a revision?
- What is new scope?
- How are extra requests billed?

Ambiguous revision policies create conflict.

---

## 7. Discuss Confidentiality

Confirm:

- NDA availability
- client contact policy
- branding rules
- portfolio rules
- credential handling

---

## 8. Start With a Test Project

Do not necessarily move your entire client pipeline on day one.

A smaller project can reveal:

- communication quality
- development quality
- adherence to scope
- turnaround
- QA quality
- revision handling

Scale the relationship after the workflow is proven.

- the client specifically requires named internal personnel
- the economics clearly favor a permanent team

White labeling is a business tool, not a universal replacement for hiring.

# Frequently Asked Questions

## What is white label web development?

White label web development is an arrangement where an external development company or developer builds websites on behalf of another agency, which then delivers the work under its own brand. The agency typically retains the client relationship while the development partner handles agreed technical work behind the scenes.

## How does white label web development work?

The agency sells and manages the client project, while a white label partner performs some or all of the development. The work is then reviewed and delivered according to the agency's client-facing process.

## Is white label web development the same as outsourcing?

White label development is a form of outsourcing, but it is specifically structured around the agency's branding and client relationship. General outsourcing does not necessarily require the external provider to remain invisible.

## Who uses white label website work?

Common users include SEO agencies, marketing agencies, design studios, consultants, freelancers, PR companies and development agencies that need additional capacity.

## What is a white label developer?

A white label web developer is a developer who performs development work on behalf of another business under an agreed white-label arrangement rather than marketing themselves directly to the end client.

## Will my clients know I use a white label developer?

That depends on the agreement and your contractual obligations. In a strict white-label arrangement, the development partner generally operates behind the agency's brand and follows agreed client-contact restrictions.

## Is white label development legal?

White labeling and subcontracting are widely used commercial arrangements. However, businesses must comply with their contracts, applicable laws, licensing obligations, confidentiality requirements and any disclosure requirements relevant to a particular engagement.

## Do white label development providers sign NDAs?

Many professional providers offer NDAs or confidentiality agreements. Agencies should confirm the exact terms before sharing confidential client information.

## Can I use white label development for WordPress?

Yes. WordPress development is a common white-label service and can include complete builds, theme customization, migrations, custom functionality, performance improvements and maintenance.

## Can WooCommerce development be white labeled?

Yes. Agencies can outsource WooCommerce development, including store setup, customization, payment integrations and other ecommerce development tasks.

## How much does white label web development cost?

There is no universal price. Cost depends on project size, technology, functionality, design complexity, integrations, ecommerce requirements, timeline and support requirements.

## Can I mark up white label development work?

Agencies generally establish their own client pricing subject to their contracts and commercial arrangements. Your pricing should account for partner costs as well as sales, project management, QA, revisions, overhead and desired margin.

## Who owns the website in a white label project?

Ownership depends on the applicable contracts and software licenses. Agencies should clearly define ownership and IP transfer with both the development partner and end client rather than assuming ownership automatically.

## What is the difference between a white label developer and a freelancer?

A freelancer is an individual working independently. A white label provider may be an individual or a larger development team, but the defining characteristic is the branded, behind-the-scenes delivery relationship. Larger providers may also offer dedicated project management, QA and greater concurrent capacity.

## How do I find a good white label development partner?

Evaluate technical expertise, portfolio quality, communication, QA, project management, confidentiality, revision policies, scalability, pricing and intellectual-property terms. Starting with a smaller test project can reduce risk.

## Can an SEO team offer web development through a white label partner?

Yes. This can allow an SEO agency to offer website builds, landing pages, technical improvements and related services without necessarily maintaining all development expertise internally.

## Is white label development good for small teams?

It can be particularly useful for smaller agencies that have development demand but cannot justify permanent specialist hires. The model can provide project-based access to technical capacity.

## Can white label development help an team scale?

It can increase delivery capacity and allow an agency to accept projects beyond its internal bandwidth. Whether it improves profitability depends on pricing, project management, partner quality, utilization and operational efficiency.

## What happens after the website launches?

Depending on the arrangement, the agency or white label partner can continue providing maintenance, updates, technical support, new landing pages and additional development.
